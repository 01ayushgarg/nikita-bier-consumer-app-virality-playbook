# 04 · The validation ladder

## The rungs

From the Gas rebuild [LP 1:00:05, 1:02:22] and his lasting lesson [LP 1:11:50]:

1. **Will people use the core flow?** (For Gas: do people send a lot of messages?)
2. **Will they spread it within their peer group?** (Within a school.)
3. **Will it hop peer groups?** (School to school.)
4. **Will people pay for it?**

> "Execute at 100% for the thing you're trying to validate at that specific stage of the
> product development cycle. And then you can kind of half-ass the rest just so you can get
> 100% signal on that one part." [LP 1:12:07]

For Gas that meant: make the polling experience "just perfect", with great questions and
working push notifications, then move to sharing and virality [LP 1:12:25 to 1:12:34].

## Conditional layers

> "If this is true, then what next needs to be true for this thing to work out? And these
> layers of conditional statements. And the more layers you have, the higher risk your product
> is, so you should try to condense it to about like four things that must be true for the
> thing to work." [LP 1:12:48]

**Checks to run:**
- Write the user's product as a chain of "if this is true, then this must be true." How many links?
  More than about four is a risk finding.
- Which is the first unproven link? That is the only thing the next test should measure.
- Is effort going into later rungs (monetization, scale) before the earlier ones hold? Flag it.

## Same idea, new climate

Gas was the tbh concept five years later, and still took "about nine launches including
renaming the app" because the growth systems had to be rebuilt [LP 59:15]. The old version
sent invite texts from a server via Twilio; by then "you really can't send texts from a server
anymore" and users had to send from their own device, which cut invites [LP 58:19 to 59:08].
**A proven rung in the past is not proven now.** Re-test it.
