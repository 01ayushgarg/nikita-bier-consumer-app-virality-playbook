# 02 · Choosing the audience

## Why teens spread apps [LP 10:48 to 11:34]

Three reasons Nikita gives:
1. **Malleable habits.** "As we get older, we get fixed into our habits of using certain
   communication products and we don't really adopt new things." [LP 10:59]
2. **They invite.** "As a user got older from age 13 to 18, the number of people that they invite
   to an app just declines almost exponentially." [LP 11:16]
3. **They see each other every day**, which he calls the most important reason. [LP 11:27]

## The invite curve

On Lenny's Podcast the **host** reads out a heuristic he credits to Nikita: for every social app
Nikita built, invitations sent per user drop 20% for every additional year of age from 13 to 18,
and if you build for adults, expect to acquire every user with ads. [LP 11:50 to 12:06] Nikita does
not restate the 20% figure himself, and we could not locate the original post, so treat the number
as Lenny's paraphrase of a post we have not seen. Nikita's own words that follow are the safer
anchor:

> "If your users aren't inviting people to your app, you're going to have to find another way to
> acquire them, and that most likely means ads." [LP 12:18]

Across 15 apps: "it was always very difficult to get the flywheel spinning for anyone after like 22
years old." [LP 16:16]

He recalls a study (he thinks from Spain) on how many people you text per year of life: rising fast
through the teens, peaking in the early twenties, then falling [LP 17:06]. The lesson he draws: "you
want to be on that upward curve of adding connections to your social graph because then the urgency
to connect is higher." [LP 18:00]

## He still says it, more bluntly

In February 2025 he wrote that when founders say they are targeting users over 18, he reminds them:
"adults have no friends." [X 2025-02-08](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/1888375654850453743)

## Look for a social inflection point

In a 2019 reply he wrote that reluctance to invite and sync contacts comes down to age, and that
without a "social inflection point", such as entering high school or college, people have no desire
to connect on new apps. [X 2019-04-02](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/1112886629910241280)

## What it means for adult apps

For adults you have to raise a lot of money for paid acquisition, and "as a seed stage up, it's
going to be basically impossible to grow that user base, especially to get density if you need
actual network effects among users." [LP 12:42]

## Density

His team "figured out ways to seed apps into schools" and later "into affinity groups, hobbyists,
things like that." [LP 19:37 to 19:41]

---

## How to apply it (our reading)

**Step 1: place the user on the curve.** Write the age band and the life moment. Score it:

| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| Entering a new school, college, city or job in the next 3 months | 2 |
| Sees the same group in person most days | 2 |
| Under about 22 | 1 |
| Already has a fixed set of daily messaging apps and no new group | 0 |

Our suggestion: 4 or more means invites can plausibly carry growth; 2 or less means plan paid or
content acquisition from day one. The scale is ours, built from the three reasons above.

**Step 2: find the dense group.** Name one real-world group (a school, a team, a club, a dorm,
hobbyists who meet) where you could get most members at once.

**Step 3: decide the channel.** If the score is low, budget for ads or one-to-many sharing
(chapters 07 and 18), not invites.

**Worked scenario (invented).** An app for new hospital nurses: adults (0 for age), but starting a
new job together (2) and seeing each other on every shift (2). Score 4. Seed one hospital's new
intake. A budgeting app for 40-year-olds scores 0: expect to pay for every user.

**Failure modes.**
- *Everyone* as the audience, so no group is ever dense.
- Treating an adult product's low invite rate as a bug to fix, rather than the expected baseline.
- Quoting the 20% figure as his measured result. It is the host's reading of a post.

**Limits and guardrails.**
- The curve is his observation across his own social apps, not a published study.
- Targeting teens brings duties: children's privacy and messaging laws, age checks, and design that
  does not pressure or shame. Read chapter 12 before you choose a minors-first audience, and get
  legal review.
