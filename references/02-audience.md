# 02 · Choosing the audience

## Why teens spread apps [LP 10:47 to 11:42]

Three reasons Nikita gives:
1. **Malleable habits.** "As we get older, we get fixed into our habits of using certain
   communication products and we don't really adopt new things."
2. **They invite.** "As a user got older from age 13 to 18, the number of people that they
   invite to an app just declines almost exponentially."
3. **They see each other every day.** Daily in-person contact is "so critical" for a network.

## The invite curve

Nikita's own heuristic, read on air by Lenny [LP 12:00]: for every social app he built, invitations
sent per user drop **20% for every additional year of age from 13 to 18**. "If you build for
adults, expect to acquire every user with ads."

Across 15 apps and every audience they tried (college, post-college): "It was always very
difficult to get the flywheel spinning for anyone after like 22 years old." [LP 16:12]

He cites an academic study (he thinks from Spain) on how many people you text per year of life:
rising fast from 14 to 18, peaking around 21, then collapsing after college and marriage, and
rising again near the end of life [LP 17:03]. "You want to be on that upward curve of adding
connections to your social graph because then the urgency to connect is higher." [LP 18:04]

## He still says it, more bluntly

[X 2025-02-08](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/1888375654850453743):

> "When founders approach me with their social app idea and tell me "we're targeting users who over
> 18," I have to remind them: adults have no friends."

## Look for a social inflection point

[X 2019-04-02](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/1112886629910241280) (a reply):

> "From my experience, reluctance to send invites & sync contacts is simply a function of age—and
> hasn't changed since the iPhone launched. If you aren't at a social inflection point (e.g., entering
> high school or college), you will have no desire to connect with people on new apps"

**Check:** is the user at a moment when they are adding people to their life (new school, new
college, new city, new job)? If not, expect low invite and contact-sync rates.

## What it means for adult apps

> "If your users aren't inviting people to your app, you're going to have to find another way
> to acquire them, and that most likely means ads." [LP 12:18]

For adults: expect to raise a lot of capital for paid acquisition, and "as a seed stage [startup]
it's going to be basically impossible to grow that user base, especially to get density if you
need actual network effects." [LP 12:30 to 12:48]

## Density

Network products need many people in one real-world group at once (a school, an affinity group,
hobbyists). His team "figured out ways to seed apps into schools" and "into affinity groups,
hobbyists." [LP 19:38]

**Checks to run:**
- What is the user's age, and where are they on the invite curve?
- Is there a real-world group whose members see each other daily?
- If adults: is there a paid-acquisition plan and the budget for it? If not, say so plainly.
- Is the product honest that it needs density, and does the launch plan create it?
