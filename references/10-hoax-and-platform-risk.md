# 10 · Hoaxes and platform risk

Nikita: any app that goes viral may get a hoax started about it, partly because warning people
about a "dangerous" app earns the poster attention and followers [LP 1:05:10 to 1:05:26]. For Gas
it was a false claim that the app was used for human trafficking, on an anonymous compliments
app with no messaging. Other founders told him they had to shut down over the same thing.

TechCrunch, at the time of the Discord deal: "The app was the subject of a widespread
sex-trafficking rumor, which was completely false... Bier told the Washington Post that he and his
team received hundreds of graphic death threats as a result of this hoax." [TC3]

## The rule

> "You really have to make sure the hoax is less viral than your app." [LP 1:09:36]

## Spot it early [LP 1:13:20 to 1:14:42]

The first sign was one support message: a screenshot of a Snapchat story, re-screenshotted by
about 40 people, plus one App Store review. Nikita told his team it would be "10 times bigger
tomorrow." The next day the App Store page was covered in such reviews.

**One support report that has already been re-shared many times is not one report.** Treat it as an incident.

## What they did [LP 1:06:25 to 1:09:31]

- **Search results:** met journalists so the top result for "Gas app human trafficking" said it
  was not. They insisted the Washington Post headline say so.
- **Retractions:** called the schools' superintendents and police chiefs who had posted the claim
  and got them to retract publicly.
- **Review bombing:** asked Apple to remove the hoax reviews.
- **Platforms:** got TikTok videos spreading the claim removed.
- **The delete flow:** the most impactful move. A TikTok video explaining the truth was shown to
  anyone deleting their account. Account deletions went from 3% of users a day at the peak to 0.1%.
- **Investors:** told interested investors that unless they could get a celebrity to post that it
  wasn't true, they weren't interested [LP 1:08:44].

## Relaunching under a new name only works once

They first renamed and relaunched on the other side of the country. The hoax followed through
a cross-state friendship, "and then it was too late at that point to relaunch again." [LP 1:14:42 to 1:15:26]

**Checks to run:**
- Is someone watching support, reviews and social mentions for the first copy of a rumour?
- What does a user see when they try to delete their account? Could it answer the rumour?
- Who would you call: press, institutions that posted it, platforms, app stores?

## The other platform risk: losing a channel

A hoax is one way growth stops overnight. A platform cutting you off is the other: Snap removed Gas
from SnapKit a week after a meeting about acquiring it [TC4]. See `07-distribution-channels.md`.
