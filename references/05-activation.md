# 05 · Activation: value in three seconds

> "If you can't demonstrate value in the first three seconds, it's over." [LP 1:27:37]

His reasoning: attention is spread across so many notifications and products. For a social
app, "you have to sign up and then the first night you have to see all of your friends on the
app and experience it, otherwise you'll churn." [LP 1:27:46]

## Every tap is a miracle

> "Every tap on a mobile app is a miracle for you as a product developer because users will turn
> and bounce to their next app very quickly." [LP 14:35]

## Invert the time to value: the Dupe case [LP 1:27:58 to 1:29:28]

- Dupe was a shopping app with many features. One feature found the cheapest version of a
  product from its page. Nikita: "That should be a whole company."
- The mechanic: type the domain in front of an existing product URL. Short domain required, so
  the founder bought Dupe.com.
- Videos went viral, users remembered the trick. By Nikita's estimate, "millions in ARR" in
  under 60 days of launching.
- He is explicit that this is not guaranteed: roughly half his advisees hit that level of
  success and half fail outright.

## Use every API, in non-traditional ways

> "Extraordinary product people are deeply aware of every possible API and how it can be used
> in non-traditional ways." [LP 1:30:30]

Example: contact sync can find all your friends AND rank the people not on the app yet by how
many friends they have on it, exposing a lot of value in one tap [LP 1:30:59].

## 10,000 taps versus one

> "A lot of founders will go and say, 'Oh, they can just exchange usernames and that's how they
> can add each other.' That is the most unrealistic thing ever." [LP 1:31:17]

Typing usernames 50 times for a 50-person friend list: "we're looking at 10,000 taps versus one." [LP 1:31:35]

## Design is the highest-ROI work

> "Consumer products live and die in the pixels. And the highest ROI you could ever have is being able to
> map out all the directions you could take this product and seeing what it would look like and picking
> the best path forward versus just kicking off engineering and adding some shiny aesthetic layer on it."
> [SOL 3:01] "Investing day one in design is something a lot of founders neglect." [SOL 3:21]

## Where installs actually happen

[X 2025-05-21](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/1925179335180197902):

> "The majority of new app installs take place in bed or on the toilet. If your app requires sound-on
> or voice to sign up, you've voluntarily accepted a 60% tax on your growth."

## Product sense is predicting conversion

He describes demoing X's new onboarding: "I was like that's going to convert at 45%. That's going
to convert at 65... I've just seen so many dashboards on these funnels that... I knew exactly what
needed to be done." [OOO 15:00]

His own definition, quoted in [X 2025-05-11](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/1921708920181055975):
"you can look at a screen and intuitively predict the percent of users who will convert to the next
screen within a 10% margin of error."

## Fix the obvious first

[X 2021-08-13](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/1426229686175027201):

> "A dumb PM will stare at an onboarding funnel for hours, fabricate problems, create months of work
> for engineers, and ultimately improve conversion by 5%. A smart PM will look at funnel in a minute,
> make a button bigger, and improve conversion by 20%."

**Checks to run:**
- Time a new user from open to first moment of real value. Over three seconds of effort before value is a finding.
- Count taps to a full friend graph. Username exchange is a red flag.
- Is there a one-line, memorable mechanic (like a URL trick) that teaches the value instantly?
- Does the first session show the user their friends already there?
- Can sign-up be finished silently, one-handed, lying down? Sound or voice in sign-up is a red flag.
- Predict each onboarding screen's conversion before looking at the data, then compare.
