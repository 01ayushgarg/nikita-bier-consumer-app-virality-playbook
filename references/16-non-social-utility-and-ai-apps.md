# 16 · Using the playbook for non-social, utility and AI apps

Most of Nikita's famous work is social apps for teens. Most founders aren't building those. This
chapter separates **what he said** (cited) from **how we apply it** (marked *Applying it*: our
reading, not his words).

## What he says carries over

> "a lot of the uh lessons and tactics to grow uh uh kind of a social network apply to many other
> products." [OOO 1:03:47]

His public advisory cases are mostly not social networks: a shopping tool (Dupe) [LP 1:28:23], a
prayer app (Bible Chat) [OOO 1:04:23], an on-demand security service (Protectors) [X 2025-02-18], a
longevity app [OOO 1:06:57], a productivity app (Saturn) [LP 1:36:16].

## 1. You probably won't reach K = 1.0. Push it up anyway

For utility apps there may not be "a path to get to uh like a full 1.0k factor" [OOO 1:05:28].
Organic distribution is still ideal "because you get genuine uh users get uh from referrals from
their peers" [OOO 1:05:43] (the captions don't make the speaker certain here).

**Applying it:** treat K as a multiplier on paid and organic acquisition, not a switch. A K of 0.4
means every 10 installs you pay for turn into about 16.7 users over time (10 ÷ (1 − 0.4)), so 6.7 of
them are free. Use `templates/03-k-factor-worksheet.md`.

## 2. Only two product channels exist

Invites (one-to-one) and sharing (one-to-many) [OOO 1:05:50].

**Applying it:** a utility app has no friend graph, so its best channel is usually **one-to-many
sharing of the result**: the thing the app produced (a price found, a plan made, a score, a
before and after). Dupe's mechanic made the result itself the shareable, memorable trick
[LP 1:28:23]. Make it the main output, not a one-off campaign (chapter 18).

## 3. Time to value matters more, not less

"if you can't demonstrate value in the first three seconds, it's over." [LP 1:27:31] The Dupe
lesson is turning one feature into the whole product and making its use obvious in one action
[LP 1:28:23 to 1:29:12].

**Applying it:** for a utility app, the first screen should *do the job*, not explain it. Sign-up
can come after the first result.

## 4. The name has to sell the download

He renamed an app because "it was hard to communicate the value proposition" and "the conversion
rates to download were really poor." [OOO 1:06:36 to 1:06:40]

**Applying it:** without friends pulling people in, the store page and the name carry the whole
conversion. Test the name against store-page-to-install conversion.

## 5. A viral video is not a growth engine

"it's kind of like a press bump. true productled growth is where uh the mechanics of the product uh
uh like are actually driving the acquisition." [OOO 1:06:14]

**Applying it:** utility founders often grow through TikTok. Track installs that come from the
product's own share and referral events separately from content spikes (`templates/01`).

## 6. AI apps: the activation moment is the first relevant response

His 2024 observation was that relevant, empathetic replies to any input are a strong activation
lever [X 2024-09-19]. More, and the guardrails, in chapter 15.

**Applying it:** for an AI app, measure time to the first response the user would call exactly
right. That is the aha moment; tune onboarding toward it.

## 7. Niches are viable now

A production app used to cost “200 $250,000”; now "a lot of smaller niche markets can have products
built for them." [OOO 1:12:16]

**Applying it:** a niche with a few hundred million in total spend can support a small, profitable
app. Size the team and the spend to the niche.

## 8. What doesn't carry over

- **The teen invite curve** (chapter 02) is about social apps. Don't conclude that adults won't use
  utilities; conclude that adults won't invite, so plan another channel.
- **Contact sync and the friendfinder** (chapter 06) matter only if the product needs your friends.
- **Seeding a whole school** (chapter 03) is a density trick. A utility needs no density; test it
  with a tightly targeted audience instead and judge it on activation and retention.
- **The 48-hour read** assumes daily use. A weekly-use utility needs two to four weeks to read
  retention (our suggestion).
