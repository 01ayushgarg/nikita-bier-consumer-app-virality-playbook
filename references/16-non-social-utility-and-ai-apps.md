# 16 · Using the playbook for non-social, utility and AI apps

Most of Nikita's famous work is social apps for teens. Most founders aren't building those. This
chapter separates **what he said** (cited) from **how we apply it** (marked "Applying it", our own
inference, not his words).

## What he says carries over

> "What I learned was like you know a lot of the uh lessons and tactics to grow uh uh kind of a social
> network apply to many other products." [OOO 1:03:43]

His public advisory cases are mostly not social networks: a shopping tool (Dupe) [LP 1:28:38], a prayer
app (Bible Chat) [OOO 1:04:20], an on-demand security service (Protectors) [X 2025-02-18], a longevity
app [OOO 1:06:48], a productivity app (Saturn) [LP 1:36:11].

## 1. You probably won't reach K = 1.0. Push it up anyway

For utility apps "there isn't necessarily a path to get to uh like a full 1.0k factor" [OOO 1:05:16].
Organic distribution is still ideal "because you get genuine uh users get uh from referrals from their
peers" [OOO 1:05:38] (the captions don't make the speaker certain here).

**Applying it:** treat K as a multiplier on paid and organic acquisition, not a switch. A K of 0.4
means every 10 installs you pay for turn into about 16.7 users over time (10 ÷ (1 − 0.4)), so 6.7 of them
are free. Use
`templates/03-k-factor-worksheet.md`.

## 2. Only two product channels exist

Invites (one-to-one) and sharing (one-to-many) [OOO 1:05:46].

**Applying it:** a utility app has no friend graph, so its best channel is usually **one-to-many
sharing of the result**: the thing the app produced (a price found, a plan made, a score, a
before/after). Dupe's mechanic made the result itself the shareable, memorable trick [LP 1:28:38].

## 3. Time to value matters more, not less

"If you can't demonstrate value in the first three seconds, it's over." [LP 1:27:37] The Dupe
lesson is turning one feature into the whole product and making its use obvious in one action
[LP 1:27:58 to 1:29:28].

**Applying it:** for a utility app, the first screen should *do the job*, not explain it. Signup
can come after the first result.

## 4. The name has to sell the download

He renamed an app because "it was hard to communicate the value proposition... the conversion rates to
download were really poor." [OOO 1:06:40]

**Applying it:** without friends pulling people in, the store page and the name carry the whole
conversion. Test the name against store-page-to-install conversion.

## 5. A viral video is not a growth engine

"It's kind of like a press bump. True productled growth is where uh the mechanics of the product uh uh
like are actually driving the acquisition." [OOO 1:06:14]

**Applying it:** utility founders often grow through TikTok. Track installs that come from the
product's own share and referral events separately from content spikes (`templates/01`).

## 6. AI apps: the activation moment is the first relevant response

"The ability to craft relevant, empathetic responses to any user input is a powerful lever to
activating users." [X 2024-09-19] More in `15-ai-era.md`.

**Applying it:** for an AI app, measure time to the first response the user would call "that's
exactly it". That is the aha moment; tune onboarding toward it.

## 7. Niches are viable now

A production app "would have cost, you know, 200 $250,000"; now "a lot of smaller niche markets can
have products built for them." [OOO 1:12:12]

**Applying it:** a niche with a few hundred million in total spend can support a small, profitable
app. Size the team and the spend to the niche.

## 8. What doesn't carry over

- **The teen invite curve** (chapter 02) is about social apps. Don't conclude "adults won't use
  utilities"; conclude "adults won't invite, so plan another channel".
- **Contact sync and the friendfinder** (chapter 06) matter only if the product needs your friends.
- **Seeding a whole school** (chapter 03) is a density trick. A utility needs no density; test it with
  a tightly targeted audience instead and judge it on activation and retention.
